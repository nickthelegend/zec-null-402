Voided in the live runs

Eight tickets that were not honoured.

Seven are the gateway's own answers in the Oct 20 run; the last is a client refusal in pass 2.

  • VOIDNULLIFIER_SPENT

    A paid proof sent again for a new request.

  • VOIDNULLIFIER_SPENT

    The second fixture note's proof replayed.

  • VOIDNOT_BOUND_TO_CHALLENGE

    The amount in the public signals changed.

  • VOIDNOT_BOUND_TO_CHALLENGE

    A proof made for one request sent for another.

  • VOIDBAD_PROOF

    The nullifier changed, claiming a different note.

  • VOID409 already claimed

    The same deposit claimed a second time.

  • VOIDMEMO_MISMATCH

    A claim with one note's value altered, on a fresh gateway.

  • VOIDANCHOR_COMPETING

    A second signed root for the same 6 leaves on the anchor address (pass 2).

Fixed on Oct 21

A review found two ways to pay twice. Both are closed.

  • A spent note's nullifier written 0<n> or in hex: now MALFORMED_SIGNALS.
  • One payment sent several times at once: now paid once.
  • A server relaying the gateway's challenge: the client refuses to prove.
  • Anchoring over HTTP: needs the operator's token.

Tested on testnet with real proofs. The review.

Fixed on Oct 22

A gateway can no longer hand a client its own root without putting it on-chain.

  • A root with no on-chain anchor: the client won't prove (UNANCHORED_ROOT).
  • A per-client root passed off as the real anchor: ANCHOR_MISMATCH.
  • An anchor older than 2,880 blocks: ANCHOR_TOO_OLD.
  • A second root signed with the gateway's key on the anchor address: ANCHOR_COMPETING.
  • A restart keeps spent nullifiers and outstanding challenges; two gateway processes on one database pay a note once.

Tested against dishonest gateways in front of a real one, with real proofs.

Read the back of the ticket

Conditions of carriage.

What a ticket hides, and who you still trust.

  1. The gateway is trusted for liveness and its nullifier set, and must keep anchoring, or notes stop paying after 2,880 blocks.
  2. Roots are anchored, addresses are not. Pinned clients refuse a second signed root; the gateway can still time anchors and sees each caller's address. The Oct 20 anchor is unsigned.
  3. Anchors count paid calls. At low traffic that times single calls to within about 10 minutes.
  4. Lightwalletd is trusted for anchors. No block header or Merkle path is checked, and it sees the client's reads.
  5. New notes wait for an anchor. In the Oct 22 live run the first call paid 2 min 44 s after the deposit was sent: the deposit was mined in 21 s.
  6. The anonymity set is the credited notes of that price inside the latest anchor. Both testnet runs had one depositor, so every call came from that depositor's notes.
  7. No change. A note pays one call of its full value; a dearer endpoint needs a note of its own price.
  8. The demo viewing key is public. Anyone can read the demo deposit's amount and memo.
  9. Single-party Groth16 setup. A real deployment needs a ceremony.
  10. Testnet only, not audited. A portfolio piece; the Zecathon entry is Kage (Shielded x402).
